The Wyckoff method, explained

Over a hundred years ago Richard Wyckoff described how large players build and unload positions. The patterns he mapped, accumulation, distribution and the spring, still show up on today’s charts.

By The Trade VaultUpdated 17 Nov 2026
Key takeaways
  • Wyckoff described four phases: accumulation, markup, distribution and markdown.
  • Accumulation is a range where large players buy without pushing price up too early.
  • The spring is a false break below the range that shakes out sellers before markup.
  • Distribution is the mirror image, with an upthrust as the false break above.

Who was Richard Wyckoff?

Richard D. Wyckoff was an American trader and educator in the early 1900s. He studied how the biggest operators of his day moved markets and concluded that price, volume and time together reveal what large players are doing. His work is still taught because the behaviour he described hasn’t changed.

The four market phases

PhaseWhat’s happening
AccumulationAfter a fall, price moves sideways while large players quietly buy.
MarkupSupply is absorbed and price trends up.
DistributionAfter a rise, price moves sideways while large players sell into strength.
MarkdownDemand runs out and price trends down.

The trends are where money is made. The ranges are where you work out which trend is coming next.

Inside an accumulation range

Wyckoff named the events inside a range. You don’t need to memorise every one, but these matter:

  • Selling climax (SC): a sharp, high-volume drop where panicked sellers hand over to buyers.
  • Automatic rally (AR): the bounce after the climax. The SC low and AR high roughly mark the range.
  • Secondary tests (ST): price revisits the lows on lower volume, a sign selling pressure is fading.
  • Spring: a break below the range that quickly reverses back inside (see below).
  • Sign of strength (SOS): a strong move up through the range, often on rising volume.

The spring

The spring is the most tradeable part of the pattern. Price drops below the bottom of the range, everyone who bought the range gets stopped out and breakout sellers jump in, and then price snaps back inside. Those stops gave large players the liquidity they needed.

It’s the same idea as a liquidity sweep. The spring is often the last shakeout before markup, which is why the Advanced Academy has a lesson called “The last spring”.

Distribution and the upthrust

Distribution is accumulation upside down: a range after a rally, a buying climax, tests of the highs on fading volume, and an upthrust, a false break above the range that traps breakout buyers before markdown.

Using Wyckoff in your trading

  • Use it to read context: is this range more likely accumulation or distribution?
  • Watch volume: climaxes come with high volume, tests with lower volume.
  • Look for the spring or upthrust as your location, then wait for a trigger and set your stop beyond the false break.
  • Don’t force it. Plenty of ranges don’t follow the textbook.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Wyckoff method?

A way of reading markets developed by Richard Wyckoff, based on how large players accumulate and distribute positions, using price, volume and time.

What is a Wyckoff spring?

A false break below an accumulation range that quickly reverses back inside. It shakes out sellers and often comes just before price trends up.

What are the four Wyckoff phases?

Accumulation, markup, distribution and markdown.

Does the Wyckoff method still work?

The behaviour it describes, large players building and unloading positions, still happens. Like any method, it gives you context and probabilities, not certainty.
