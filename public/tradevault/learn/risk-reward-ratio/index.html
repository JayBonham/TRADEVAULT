The risk-to-reward ratio, explained

Risk-to-reward compares what you stand to lose on a trade with what you stand to make. It decides how often you need to be right, which is why it matters more than any single entry.

By The Trade VaultUpdated 17 Nov 2026
Key takeaways
  • Risk-to-reward = distance to your stop compared with distance to your target.
  • At 1:2 you only need to win more than 1 in 3 trades to come out ahead, before costs.
  • Expectancy (win rate × average win − loss rate × average loss) tells you if a strategy pays over time.
  • Set the stop and target before you enter, and don’t move the stop further away.

What is the risk-to-reward ratio?

Every trade has two exits: your stop loss, where you’re wrong, and your target, where you take profit. The risk-to-reward ratio (R:R) compares the two.

If your stop is 40 points away and your target is 80 points away, you’re risking 40 to make 80. That’s 1:2. Traders often call the amount risked “1R”, so a winner at 1:2 makes 2R and a loser costs 1R.

The win rate you need

Here’s why R:R matters so much. The bigger your reward compared with your risk, the less often you need to be right to break even:

Risk : rewardBreak-even win rateOut of 10 trades
1 : 150%5 winners to break even
1 : 1.540%4 winners
1 : 233.3%a little over 3 winners
1 : 325%2.5 winners

The formula is simple: break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + reward). At 1:2 that’s 1 ÷ 3 = 33.3%. These numbers are before spreads and commission, which push them up slightly.

Expectancy: does your strategy pay?

R:R on its own isn’t enough. A 1:5 trade that only wins 10% of the time still loses money. What you want to know is your expectancy: the average result per trade.

Expectancy (in R) = win rate × average win − loss rate × average loss

Say you win 45% of trades at 1:2. That’s 0.45 × 2 − 0.55 × 1 = +0.35R per trade on average. Over 100 trades risking $100 each, that’s about $3,500 before costs. Your trading journal is where you get the real win rate to put in here.

What about inverse risk-to-reward?

Some strategies deliberately use a target smaller than the stop, for example 2:1 risk to reward. That only works with a high, proven win rate (above 66.7% at 2:1, before costs). It’s a legitimate approach, and it’s covered in the Advanced Academy, but it’s unforgiving: a run of losses hurts more, so it needs strict rules and real data behind it.

Rules that protect your R:R

  • Decide both exits before you enter. If you can’t name the stop and target, there’s no trade.
  • Never widen your stop. Moving it further away turns a 1:2 trade into 1:1 or worse.
  • Put stops where you’re actually wrong, beyond a level, not at a random number of points.
  • Size from the stop. Use a position size calculator so 1R is always the same amount of money.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good risk-to-reward ratio?

Many traders aim for at least 1:2, because it lets you be profitable while winning only a little over a third of your trades (before costs). The right ratio depends on your strategy’s actual win rate.

How do I calculate risk-to-reward?

Divide the distance from entry to target by the distance from entry to stop. A 40-point stop and an 80-point target is 80 ÷ 40 = 2, so 1:2.

Is a higher risk-to-reward always better?

No. Larger targets get hit less often. What matters is expectancy: win rate multiplied by average win, minus loss rate multiplied by average loss.

What does 1R mean?

1R is the amount you risk on a trade. If you risk $100, a 2R winner makes $200 and a full loss costs 1R, $100.
